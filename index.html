Thirst

8 kHz wired & wireless controller

Printable PDF version

Most high-speed controllers get their speed by cutting something. Some swap adaptive triggers and HD haptics for basic rumble motors. Some read sticks and triggers at 8 bits, which is 256 steps from rest to full tilt. Some advertise 8,000 Hz but only check your buttons 1,000 times a second, then wait a few milliseconds to confirm each press. Others drop the touchpad or motion controls, or go wireless at Bluetooth speeds.

Thirst doesn't make that trade. It keeps the adaptive triggers, HD haptics, touchpad, motion controls and lights, adds eight extra buttons, and runs at 8,000 Hz wired and wireless. Sticks and triggers are read at 16 bits, 65,536 steps, and every report carries a brand-new reading taken just before your PC collects it. Two inner layers of solid copper shield the sensitive circuits from the radio, motors and haptics.

It is also a specialised low-power design. Every feature can be switched off when you don't need it. Turn off rumble, the adaptive triggers and the lights, and it runs for 50+ hours on a stock DualSense battery, even at 8,000 Hz over the dongle with motion controls still on.

Latency: press to PC

Latency is how long after you press a button or move a stick the PC has it. It includes everything the controller does along the way: scanning, confirming the press, processing, and the radio hop when wireless. Polling rate alone tells you none of this. Many controllers report at 8,000 Hz and still take milliseconds to confirm a button press. This one doesn't wait.

Measured, not just calculated

We test the full pipeline, from the instant a button closes to the moment the input arrives at your PC, not just the gap between reports. A custom tester presses the controller electrically on a precise schedule and timestamps every stage along the way, to a precision current latency testers don't reach. It also records internal timing that ordinary testers can't see. Our goal is simple: the lowest true press-to-PC latency of any controller.

Report rate isn't freshness

Two controllers can both say 8,000 Hz and deliver very different data. Many scan their buttons and sticks only 1,000 times a second, then send the same reading in eight reports in a row. Their data can be a full millisecond old, even though it arrives 8,000 times a second. Others send 8,000 reports on their own clock, which slowly drifts against your PC's. A report can sit waiting for the PC to ask for it, and every so often two land at once and one is lost, or none arrives and the last one repeats.

Thirst stays in step with your PC. Every input, every button, stick and trigger, is captured in time with your PC's own collection timing, so each report carries the newest reading the controller could have sent. Never a repeat, never a gap.

Measurement and signal quality

In-frame averaging

Sticks and triggers are sampled 384,000 times a second, and each reading is averaged inside a single 8,000 Hz report cycle, before it reaches the processor. Every report carries a new, true 16-bit reading: never repeated, never upscaled, with no extra processor load, leaving its full attention for timing and the radio. Other controllers average across several reports, so their readings are already old when they're sent. Thirst's are a fraction of a report old, measured from the moment of sampling.

Locked-reference sensing

Stick and trigger readings don't shift with battery level, with switching between battery, USB and dock power, with different ports and cables, or with the load from rumble and the adaptive triggers. Centre and full travel stay where they were calibrated, so your deadzones and curves feel the same in hour one and hour ten.

Digital from the start

Sticks and triggers are converted to digital on their own dedicated converter, outside the processor, so the processor's electrical noise is kept out of the reading.

Built for stability

Precision, low-drift components are used throughout the measurement and timing circuits, so readings and wireless timing hold steady across temperature and from unit to unit.

The rumble and trigger columns are the average current each feature draws from the battery at that level, on top of everything else. 100% is the most Thirst can deliver. Each rumble actuator then draws about 280–450 mA from the battery, with both together capped by the battery's ~0.9 A limit; each trigger motor draws about 270–410 mA, set by the board's 0.4 A per-motor current limit. The rumble amplifiers also draw about 5 mA whenever rumble is enabled. Rumble power rises with the square of its current, so 33% strength uses about 11% of full power; trigger current rises in step with force. A typical shooter such as Call of Duty falls between light and medium. Rumble and triggers are never at constant 100% together: firmware limits rumble while the trigger motors are pushing, to stay inside the battery's current limit.

Roadmap

The order things happen in, from pre-orders to production.

  1. Pre-orders open Very soon, likely week 1–2 of November

    Under 20 boards. A 50% deposit of the $350 maximum price reserves a board.

  2. Test board order

    The initial test boards are ordered. Initial runs take about 3–4 weeks, plus shipping.

  3. Design validation and basic firmware

    The physical design is validated on real hardware, and a basic firmware build brings up comms with the dongle.

  4. Latency testing

    Real input latency is measured with a high-speed, firmware-linked test device.

  5. First consumer prototypes ordered

    Once testing shows the boards are stable, the first round of consumer prototypes is ordered. Remaining balances are calculated from the number of boards on order, and the price per board drops as milestones are met.

  6. Production

    Firmware work continues while boards are in production. Later runs take about 2–4 weeks each, plus shipping.

Features

Controls

  • Full standard layout: D-pad, four face buttons, L1/R1, analog L2/R2 with digital end stops, L3/R3, Create, Options, Home, Mute and touchpad click
  • 8 extra buttons for back paddles or anywhere else you want them, all remappable
  • Encoder input for a scroll wheel or dial. It is decoded in hardware, so fast spins never drop steps
  • Frame Sniping: the controller tracks your PC's polling in real time and adapts to it. Every input is sampled at the last useful moment, so each report reaches the PC as fresh as it can be
  • Zero-delay debounce: a press is reported on its first edge, and contact bounce afterwards is ignored
  • Home button wakes the controller from deep sleep. There is also a rear pinhole reset

Feedback

  • HD rumble: two voice-coil actuators driven by digital amplifiers. On PC, haptics arrive as audio channels, the same way the original controller does it
  • Adaptive triggers on both sides: motor-driven resistance with about 60 force steps. Trigger position is measured, so effects like weapon kick and tension zones respond to where your finger actually is
  • The trigger motors are current-limited and report faults, so a jammed trigger shuts off safely

Touchpad and lighting

  • Touchpad: full multi-touch with click, using the original touchpad
  • Touchscreen-ready: the touchpad connector can also run a small touch display in place of the touchpad. The board detects which one is fitted at power-up
  • Player LEDs on the touchpad, three segments, each dimmable separately
  • Full-colour RGB status light and a dimmable mic/mute light

Power and charging

  • Fits the stock battery, with temperature-monitored smart charging
  • Built-in fuel gauge for an accurate battery percentage
  • Wireless-charging ready: a protected 5 V input for charging dock contacts or a wireless charging receiver. It is reverse-blocked and surge-protected
  • Storage mode cuts the drain to almost nothing; press Home to wake it

Companion app In development

  • PC and phone app for button remapping, including the 8 extras
  • Stick curves and deadzones; trigger curves and click points
  • Report rate selectable from 125 Hz to 8,000 Hz
  • Adaptive trigger profiles, rumble strength, gyro sensitivity and lighting
  • Always in sync: change a setting from the PC or the phone and the other updates to match. The controller holds the settings
  • Built-in latency self-test that checks the controller's own timing
  • Cable and port test: checks your USB cable and port for a stable link and their effective speed at 8,000 Hz, with a simple pass, marginal or fail result and details for those who want them
  • Profiles stored on the controller. Firmware updates over USB

Build

  • Drop-in board for the DualSense shell. Stick, button and connector positions are kept
  • Expansion boards: the trigger motors, haptics, shoulder buttons and extra buttons connect through removable expansion boards. More expansion boards are planned
  • USB-C with through-hole anchoring for cable-yank strength, and ESD protection on the data lines
  • 4-layer board with an impedance-controlled USB pair, for a clean High-Speed link
  • Antenna kept clear of copper at the board edge, for range in the stock shell

Planned Future firmware

  • Controller as dongle: plug one Thirst into your PC and it acts as the wireless receiver for a second one, at full 8 kHz

Not in this revision

  • 3.5 mm headset jack, built-in speaker and microphone
  • PS5 console play. This revision is PC-first; console support would need a future dongle
